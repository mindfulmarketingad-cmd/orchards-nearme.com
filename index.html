Pick-Your-Own Orchards & Farms Across All 50 States

Whether you're chasing the first blush of summer berries or the crisp snap of a fall apple, America's orchards and farms are open for picking. This directory maps thousands of apple picking orchards, cherry picking farms, berry picking spots, peach orchards, blueberry farms, and garden centers so you can find one near you in seconds.

🍎 Apple Picking Season

Peak apple picking runs late August through October across most of the country. The Northeast and Midwest lead the way — think Honeycrisp in Minnesota, Cortland in New England, and Fuji in the Pacific Northwest. Southern states like Georgia and North Carolina see earlier harvests starting in July. Look for u-pick signs along rural routes or use the map above to find orchards by ZIP code.

🍒 Cherry Picking Season

Cherry picking is one of the shortest seasons of the year — sweet cherries are typically ripe for just 2–3 weeks in June and July. Michigan's Traverse City region, Oregon's Hood River Valley, and Washington State produce the most abundant pick-your-own cherry orchards. Tart (sour) cherries used for pies follow a week or two behind sweet varieties.

🍓 Berry Picking Season

Berry season stretches from late spring all the way to fall depending on the variety. Strawberries kick things off in May across Southern states, moving north through June. Blueberries peak July–August in the Mid-Atlantic and New England. Raspberries and blackberries follow in late summer. Find berry picking farms near you with our interactive map, or browse blueberry picking locations by state.

🍑 Peach Picking Season

Nothing beats a sun-warm peach pulled straight from the tree. Peach picking peaks in July and August across Georgia, South Carolina, and the mid-Atlantic states, with California and Colorado picking running June through September. Freestone varieties — easiest for eating fresh — ripen in the heart of summer, while clingstone types used for canning arrive first. Find roadside peach orchards on our map.

Frequently Asked Questions About Orchards Near Me

How do I find orchards near me?

The fastest way to find orchards near me is to enter your ZIP code in the search bar at the top of this page. Our interactive map instantly plots every orchard, pick-your-own farm, and garden center within driving distance, along with ratings and real visitor reviews so you can pick the best one before you go. You can also browse the full directory or jump straight to a specific fruit, like apple picking orchards near me or cherry picking orchards near me.

What is the best time of year to visit an orchard near me?

It depends on what you want to pick. Most apple orchards near me peak from late August through October, cherry orchards have a short window in June and July, and berry and peach picking generally runs from late spring through summer. Our seasonal picking guides break down exact harvest windows for apples state by state, so you can time your visit to hit peak ripeness.

Are orchards near me free to visit, or is there an entrance fee?

Most orchards near me are free to enter and only charge for the fruit you pick, usually by the pound or per bag. Some larger operations charge a small admission or parking fee that covers extras like hayrides, corn mazes, or petting zoos. Fees vary by farm, so check the listing's website or call ahead before you go — every listing on our map includes a link to get directions and visit the orchard's website.

What should I bring when visiting an orchard near me?

Wear closed-toe shoes and clothes you don't mind getting a little dirty, since orchard rows can be muddy after rain. Bring cash, since many smaller orchards near me aren't set up for card payments at the picking stand. A light jacket helps for cool morning visits, and a cooler in the car keeps your haul fresh on the drive home. Sunscreen and water are essentials for summer berry and peach picking.

Can I bring my kids and pets to orchards near me?

Most orchards near me are family-friendly and welcome kids, with many offering extras like hayrides, petting zoos, and play areas built for a full family day out. Pet policies vary a lot more by farm — some working orchards allow leashed dogs, while others don't due to livestock or food-safety rules. Check the individual listing or call the orchard directly to confirm before bringing a pet along.

What's the difference between an orchard, a farm, and a garden center?

An orchard grows tree fruit like apples, cherries, and peaches, usually for pick-your-own visits. A farm is a broader category that can include berry fields, pumpkin patches, and other seasonal produce alongside or instead of tree fruit. A garden center is a retail nursery that sells plants, flowers, and gardening supplies rather than offering picking. Our map lets you filter by all three so you can find exactly the kind of visit you're looking for.

How do I know if an orchard near me offers pick-your-own?

Every listing on our map shows which fruits are available to pick, with tags like Apple Picking, Cherry Picking, or Berry Picking directly on the card and map popup. Use the filter chips above the map to narrow it down to only orchards that offer the specific fruit you're after, then check ratings and recent reviews to see what other visitors picked and how their trip went.

Do orchards near me take reservations, or can I just show up?

Policies vary widely. Many smaller, family-run orchards near me are walk-in only, especially on weekdays. Larger or more popular operations — particularly during peak fall apple season — often require online reservations or timed-entry tickets to manage crowds, especially on weekends. Checking the orchard's website (linked from every listing) before you drive out is the safest way to avoid a wasted trip.
