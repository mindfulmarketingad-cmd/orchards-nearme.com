Cherry Picking Season North Dakota

July 1, 2025 · Orchards Near Me

Cherry picking season in North Dakota is july through August for the hardy sour cherry varieties grown in North Dakota, with the season running late due to the northern climate and brief summers.

Typical season: July through August for the hardy sour cherry varieties grown in North Dakota, with the season running late due to the northern climate and brief summers. Exact timing shifts a week or two earlier or later depending on the year's weather.

Where to Pick Cherries in North Dakota

Cherry picking near Bismarck is a genuinely rare and limited activity, as North Dakota's continental climate is challenging for most cherry varieties to bear consistently. The hardy sour cherry cultivars developed for northern growing conditions do exist in the state, but the commercial pick-your-own operations for cherry picking are scarce and typically very small in scale. This scarcity makes finding a North Dakota cherry operation a small adventure worth undertaking.

Use the map on our cherry picking near Bismarck, North Dakota page to find specific farms by ZIP code and read visitor reviews before you go.

Best Cherry Varieties in North Dakota

North Dakota orchards and farms commonly grow tart cherry varieties such as Montmorency, especially in the Great Lakes region, alongside a smaller sweet cherry crop. Availability varies by farm and time of season, so ask the farm staff which rows are currently at peak — that guidance is worth more than any printed list.

Tips for Cherry Picking in North Dakota

For Bismarck residents interested in cherry picking, Minnesota's orchard belt accessible on I-94 east offers the most practical options—the drive to the Twin Cities area or continuing further to Wisconsin's orchard regions is a viable weekend trip that connects Great Plains residents to the fruit-growing culture of the Upper Midwest.

General tips that apply everywhere: bring your own containers or use the farm's, wear clothes you don't mind staining, dress in layers since mornings can start cool, and bring cash — many smaller farms are not set up for card payments at the stand.
