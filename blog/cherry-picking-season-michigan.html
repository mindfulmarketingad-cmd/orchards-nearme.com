Cherry Picking Season Michigan

July 1, 2025 · Orchards Near Me

Cherry picking season in Michigan is late June through July, with the Leelanau and Old Mission Peninsula tart cherry harvest typically at peak in the last week of June and first week of July.

Typical season: Late June through July, with the Leelanau and Old Mission Peninsula tart cherry harvest typically at peak in the last week of June and first week of July. Exact timing shifts a week or two earlier or later depending on the year's weather.

Where to Pick Cherries in Michigan

Lansing sits within striking distance of the cherry capital of the United States, with the Traverse City area—about three hours north—famous worldwide for its tart cherry production. Michigan grows approximately 75 percent of the nation's tart cherry crop, and the orchards of the Leelanau and Old Mission peninsulas welcome pick-your-own visitors in late June and early July for one of the most celebrated fruit-picking events in the Midwest.

Use the map on our cherry picking near Lansing, Michigan page to find specific farms by ZIP code and read visitor reviews before you go.

Best Cherry Varieties in Michigan

Michigan orchards and farms commonly grow tart cherry varieties such as Montmorency, especially in the Great Lakes region, alongside a smaller sweet cherry crop. Availability varies by farm and time of season, so ask the farm staff which rows are currently at peak — that guidance is worth more than any printed list.

Tips for Cherry Picking in Michigan

Traverse City cherry season is one of Michigan's most celebrated annual events, and residents of Lansing who haven't made the three-hour drive north should put it on their calendar. Late June in Leelanau County combines cherry picking with some of Michigan's most beautiful landscapes—the Old Mission and Leelanau Peninsulas, Lake Michigan vistas, and wine country that rivals anything in the Midwest.

General tips that apply everywhere: bring your own containers or use the farm's, wear clothes you don't mind staining, dress in layers since mornings can start cool, and bring cash — many smaller farms are not set up for card payments at the stand.
