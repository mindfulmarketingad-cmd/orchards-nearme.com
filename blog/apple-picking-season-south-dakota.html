Apple Picking Season South Dakota

July 1, 2025 · Orchards Near Me

Apple picking season in South Dakota is concentrated in September, with the Black Hills in the west and the eastern farmland producing limited but genuine u-pick opportunities with cold-hardy varieties.

When Is Apple Picking Season in South Dakota?

South Dakota's climate is challenging for apple growing — extreme cold winters, spring hail, and hot summers require specialized varieties to survive and produce. The Black Hills have a slightly more temperate microclimate than the surrounding plains, and a handful of orchards operate there. Cold-hardy varieties from the University of Minnesota breeding program — Haralson, Honeygold — are the staples of South Dakota orchards.

Where to Pick Apples in South Dakota

The best apple picking in South Dakota is concentrated in the Black Hills near Rapid City and Custer, the Sioux Falls area in the east, and scattered farms across the eastern tier. These areas combine the climate, soil, and agricultural tradition that makes pick-your-own orchards viable and worthwhile as a destination.

Best Apple Varieties in South Dakota

South Dakota orchards commonly grow Haralson, Honeygold, and cold-hardy northern varieties. Availability varies by farm and time of season — earlier varieties like Gala and Zestar kick things off, while Honeycrisp, Fuji, and later-ripening types extend the season toward its close. When you arrive, ask the farm staff which rows are currently at peak — that guidance is worth more than any printed list.

Tips for Apple Picking in South Dakota

South Dakota apple picking is a regional experience worth doing if you are visiting the Black Hills in September, but do not make a special trip expecting a classic orchard experience without checking listings and confirming operations first.

General tips that apply everywhere: bring a bag or use the farm's containers, wear closed-toe shoes (fallen apples make the ground soft and uneven), dress in layers since orchard mornings can be cool even in September, and bring cash — many smaller farms are not set up for card payments at the stand.
