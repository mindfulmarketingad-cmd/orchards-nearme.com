Cherry Picking Season South Dakota

July 1, 2025 · Orchards Near Me

Cherry picking season in South Dakota is july through August for the Black Hills cherry orchards, with the season running later than in neighboring states due to the high elevation and continental climate.

Typical season: July through August for the Black Hills cherry orchards, with the season running later than in neighboring states due to the high elevation and continental climate. Exact timing shifts a week or two earlier or later depending on the year's weather.

Where to Pick Cherries in South Dakota

Cherry picking in the Pierre area is limited, but the Black Hills region to the west harbors some cherry orchards in its sheltered valleys and among its ponderosa forests. Sour cherry varieties are more common than sweet in South Dakota, and the pick-your-own season, where it exists, runs in July when the high plains finally warm enough to bring the cherries to ripeness. Finding these orchards is part of the adventure.

Use the map on our cherry picking near Pierre, South Dakota page to find specific farms by ZIP code and read visitor reviews before you go.

Best Cherry Varieties in South Dakota

South Dakota orchards and farms commonly grow tart cherry varieties such as Montmorency, especially in the Great Lakes region, alongside a smaller sweet cherry crop. Availability varies by farm and time of season, so ask the farm staff which rows are currently at peak — that guidance is worth more than any printed list.

Tips for Cherry Picking in South Dakota

South Dakota cherry picking is limited enough that finding a farm requires research and direct outreach—look for listings through state agriculture directories and be prepared for small operations that may require advance scheduling. The Black Hills orchards that do grow cherries in their sheltered draws produce fruit with a character shaped by the high plains climate that is worth experiencing.

General tips that apply everywhere: bring your own containers or use the farm's, wear clothes you don't mind staining, dress in layers since mornings can start cool, and bring cash — many smaller farms are not set up for card payments at the stand.
