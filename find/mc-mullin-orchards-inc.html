★★☆☆☆David Rowley4 years ago

After years of being delighted with the Diamond Princess peach variety, this year's crop (August 2021) was particularly disappointing. First of all, it cost several dollars more for a box of #1's, but their ripening quality was substandard. Peaches are always a bit tricky to ripen properly, but with Diamond Princess, we felt ok getting maybe 50% of the box to ripen perfectly, by taking a few out of the fridge at a time to ripen, then when the fruit would slightly yield to pressure from one's thumb, and it smelled fragrant, there was at least a 50% chance it would be soft to bite, very juicy, with clear yellow flesh. This year (2021), I felt lucky if 1 out of 20 came out well. As they ripened at room temperature, a lot of blemishes and dark spots would appear. When they finally were fully red, fragrant, and yielded to gentle pressure from the thumb, I would cut them open. Nearly without fail, they were shading towards brown on the inside (i.e., past their prime) but still CRUNCHY, with a rubbery, cucumber-like consistency!!! A ripe peach should not crunch at all, but be a soft, nearly noiseless bite. Fewer than 1 in 20 of these 2021 Diamond Princess peaches were of acceptable eating quality, but I paid a much higher price than for them, than in previous years. 9/24/21 - Bought a box of Summer Lady after the failed Diamond Princess box. The Summer Lady have been everything the Diamond Princess was not. Hardly a bad one in the lot: clear dark yellow flesh, juicy, fragrant and sweet.
