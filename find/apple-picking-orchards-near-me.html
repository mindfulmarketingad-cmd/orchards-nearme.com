Why Apple Picking Is the Fall Tradition I Look Forward to All Year

I have been chasing apple season since I was old enough to reach the lower branches of a Honeycrisp tree. There is something about pulling a firm, sun-warmed apple off the branch yourself that a grocery store bag will never come close to matching. The smell alone, that clean mix of fruit and fresh air and just a little bit of earth, is enough to reset an entire week. If you have never done it before, or if it has been years since your last visit to an orchard, I hope this guide helps you find an apple picking spot near you and makes the most of your trip.

What to Expect at an Apple Picking Orchard

Most pick-your-own orchards work pretty much the same way. You arrive, pay a small admission or bag fee, grab a bag or a basket, and head out into the rows. Some orchards have wagon rides out to the picking area; others let you walk straight in. Either way, you are going to want comfortable shoes you do not mind getting a little dirty and layers you can peel off because orchard mornings can be surprisingly cool even in September.

The staff will usually point you toward the varieties that are ready that day. This matters more than people realize. Not every apple is in season at the same time, and a good orchard will have staggered plantings so something is always coming ripe from late summer all the way through October and sometimes into November. When I visited an orchard in upstate New York one September, I went expecting Galas and left with a bag of Macouns I had never even tried before. That surprise is part of what makes going to an orchard so much better than shopping.

The Best Apple Varieties to Pick and What to Do With Them

I get asked this a lot: which apple is the best to pick? The honest answer is that it depends on what you plan to do with them. Here is what I have learned from years of going to orchards across the country.

For eating out of hand, Honeycrisp is the crowd favorite and for good reason. It is sweet, juicy, and has that satisfying crunch that feels almost dramatic. Fuji is another reliable choice if you prefer something a little sweeter and denser. I also have a soft spot for Cortland, which is a bit more tart and holds up beautifully without browning as fast as other varieties when you slice it.

For pies and baking, I always reach for something with more acid to balance the sugar in the recipe. Golden Delicious softens beautifully. Jonagold gives you a good mix of sweet and tart. My personal favorite for pie is a blend, half Granny Smith for structure and bite, half Braeburn for sweetness and that jammy quality that makes the filling smell incredible while it bakes.

For cider and pressing, if your orchard has a press or sells fresh cider, ask which varieties they use. A good cider apple is often one that would be considered too sharp or too tannic to eat fresh, and that complexity is exactly what makes the juice so layered and interesting. Winesap, Northern Spy, and Kingston Black are classics for a reason.

Just type your ZIP code into the search bar and the results will sort by distance so you can see what is closest. I recommend calling ahead before you make the trip, especially on weekday mornings early in the season when some orchards have limited hours or require reservations for large groups.

A few things worth checking before you go: whether the orchard requires cash only, what the bag or bucket fee covers, whether dogs are allowed on the property, and whether there is a covered area or picnic space in case the weather turns. The reviews on the listings will often answer these questions, and many orchards have websites with updated seasonal information.

Going With Kids: What Makes an Orchard Visit Actually Fun for Families

I have taken my kids to orchards where they lasted about fifteen minutes before they were bored and sticky. I have also taken them to orchards where we stayed for three hours and they still did not want to leave. The difference almost always comes down to what else the orchard offers beyond the picking itself.

The best family orchards have a few things in common. There is usually something to do while you wait, a corn maze, a petting zoo, a hayride, or a play area. There is food, specifically something warm and sweet, because nothing marks the end of a good orchard visit like a bag of fresh cider donuts or a cup of hot apple cider. And the picking area itself is laid out in a way that is easy to navigate with small kids who are not great at walking in a straight line between the rows.

I always look for orchards that let younger kids pick from the lower branches of dwarf trees rather than needing to climb or reach. It keeps them engaged and gives them a real sense of having done the work themselves, which is the whole point. There is a particular look of satisfaction on a five-year-old's face when they put a big apple in a bag they are carrying themselves. It is one of those small moments you do not forget.

The Best Time of Year to Go Apple Picking

The peak of apple season in the United States runs from mid-August through October, though the exact window shifts depending on where you are. In the South and warmer parts of the mid-Atlantic, you can find early varieties ready in late July and August. In New England, the Great Lakes region, and the Pacific Northwest, the heart of the season is September and early October. Higher elevation orchards in the Rockies and Appalachians can run later into the fall.

The sweet spot, in my experience, is the second and third weeks of September. The air has that unmistakable autumn quality, cool and bright, without the full rush of the peak weekend crowds that come in October. The variety selection is also usually at its widest during those weeks, with early types still finishing and mid-season varieties coming into their prime at the same time.

That said, I have had wonderful apple picking visits all the way into November at some orchards that grow cold-hardy late-season varieties specifically to extend the season. If you missed the September window, do not assume it is too late. Check the listings, call the orchard, and you may be surprised.

What to Bring to an Apple Picking Orchard

Over the years I have refined what I bring to an orchard visit down to a short list that makes the whole thing more comfortable and more fun.

Wear closed-toe shoes. Fallen apples make the ground soft and uneven and sandals are a bad idea. Bring a light jacket you can tie around your waist if it warms up. Pack a small cooler if you are driving home more than an hour, because fresh-picked apples keep best when they are cold and you will probably pick more than you planned. Bring a camera or make sure your phone is charged because orchards are genuinely beautiful, especially in the morning light with dew still on the fruit. And bring cash, because a lot of smaller orchards are not set up for card payments at the stand.

One more thing I always bring: an empty stomach. Orchard food, whether it is fresh cider, apple fritters, caramel apples, or a simple slice of pie sold warm from a farmstand counter, is almost always better than you expect. Part of what makes apple picking such a complete experience is that it engages all your senses at once. Save room for it.

Supporting Local Orchards

Most of the orchards on this map are family-owned and have been operating for generations. They are small agricultural businesses that depend on seasonal visitors to stay viable, and the pressures they face, from rising land costs to unpredictable weather to competition from large commercial growers, are real. When you visit a local orchard and buy their cider or their jam or a bag of apples you could have gotten cheaper at a supermarket, you are doing something that matters beyond the afternoon itself. You are keeping a piece of working agricultural land in production and a family business running.

I think about that every time I pull out of an orchard parking lot with the back seat full of apples and my kids already arguing about who picked the biggest one. It is a good feeling, the kind that lasts longer than a single afternoon. Use the map above to find an apple picking orchard near you this season and go make some of those memories for yourself.
